## The defect: an invite-only retry that could not escalate

`2026-08-31 23:44:21` — a local network blip drops all three IRC connections and both
FTP pools. IRC reconnects **in-process** 75 s later and re-runs `SITE INVITE`. glftpd
accepts it (`Command Successful.`) on **both zephyr and superbnc**. No `INVITE` arrives.

The standing retry then re-issues an **accepted** `SITE INVITE` 25 more times over
**9 h 50 m** (5 m → 15 m → 30 m forever), on **two independent IRC networks**, and every
`JOIN` returns 473. All four invite-only channels — `#ent`, `#supers`, `#supers-chat`,
`#supers-spam` — stay out. superbnc's channels are the primary auto-race trigger, so the
site produced **zero announces** for the entire window.

`09:35:52` — an *unrelated* auto-update restarts the process. On the fresh session **one**
`SITE INVITE` produces all three `INVITE`s in **746 ms**, and there is not another
invite-only warning for the remaining 9.4 hours.

### What was ruled out

- **FTP control-channel desync** — `SITE STATS` parsed cleanly every 5 minutes on zephyr
  across the whole window; zero stale-reply, `Type set to A`, or CPSV-desync signatures.
  The v3.10.56 guard held.
- **Dead inbound IRC** — the 473s themselves arrived and were parsed.
- **Nick fallback** — `ResolveSiteInviteNick` logs an `[INF]` whenever it targets a
  fallback nick; that line never appeared.
- **A site-side bot outage** — two different bots on two different networks do not both
  go deaf for exactly the same 9 h 50 m and both heal at the instant *our* process
  restarts (≈0.06% coincidence over the 14-minute window).

**The cause lives past `SITE INVITE` and is not determinable from our logs.** That is
recorded as an open question rather than guessed at. What *is* determinable: repeating an
action that has already failed 25 times is not a recovery strategy, and the one action
observed to work — tearing the session down and building a new one — was never attempted.

## Changes

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `Irc/InviteRecoveryPolicy.cs` *(new)* | After 8 failed join attempts (~65 min past the first standing-retry warning) force a full IRC reconnect. Capped at one per 2 h so a genuinely uninvitable channel cannot become a reconnect loop. Replaying the real outage yields **4–6 escalations instead of 0**. |
| `Irc/IrcService.cs` | Escalation wired into `RetryJoinAfterDelay`. An accepted `SITE INVITE` that yields no `INVITE` inside the 2 s grace window now logs a WRN — previously an `[INF] Command Successful.` sat next to an unrelated-looking 473 with nothing connecting them. |
| `Irc/IrcService.cs` | `HandleNickInUseAsync` now logs 433 / nick fallback to **Serilog**, not just the in-app tab. A fallback nick silently changes the identity `SITE INVITE` targets; it was the one hypothesis this investigation could not close, purely because it left no trace in the log file. |

## Remaining risks

1. **The escalation is a mitigation, not a root-cause fix.** The reason an accepted
   `SITE INVITE` produces no `INVITE` on a reconnected session is still unknown. A forced
   reconnect is the strongest in-process approximation of the only action observed to
   work, but the 23:45 in-process reconnect *did* fail once — 75 s after the blip, when
   the far side was plausibly still settling. Confidence rests on retrying it hourly
   rather than on a proven mechanism.
2. **New instrumentation is the real closer.** The next occurrence should produce a
   `SITE INVITE was accepted … but no INVITE arrived` WRN and, if relevant, a nick-fallback
   WRN. Grep for those before theorising again.
3. **Forced reconnects are a new behaviour on a rate-limit-sensitive path.** The 2 h floor
   should keep IRC reconnects well clear of trouble, but this is worth watching — these
   are IRC networks, not the FTP BNC (whose ~2 h cooldown is untouched by this change).
4. **Unrelated and still open:** SYN's FTP host has been down since 2026-08-13 (~46
   races/day blocked, 12 remount failures in the sample window) and `loginHeadroom: 2`
   still caps FXP at 1 concurrent transfer per site. Both are Dave's calls, not code.
