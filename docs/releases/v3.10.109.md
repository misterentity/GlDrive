## Root cause and change

`FxpTransfer.ExecuteRelay` issues RETR on the source first and STOR on the destination second.
A rejected STOR threw with `FxpFaultSide.Both`, so `SpreadJob.ApplyPoisonAttribution` discarded
both pooled connections: the destination login went into a 20 s quarantine and was re-created,
against an account cap of 4 logins. But a 425 is the final reply on the destination's control
channel — nothing is outstanding and its data sequence never began. The 553 dupe-skip branch
immediately above handles that identical state by clearing the `OpenDataTcp` mark and returning
the destination to the pool, and it did so 16, 78 and 122 times on the three days with zero
desync symptoms. Only the source is entangled (an accepted RETR streaming into a socket that is
about to be closed).

The rejection branch now calls `CpsvDataHelper.EndDataSequence(dst)` and attributes
`FxpFaultSide.Source`. The other three transfer modes order STOR before RETR and already
attribute `Dest` there; Relay's attribution now likewise follows the entangled side rather than
the side that spoke. Product, README and changelog were advanced to **3.10.109**.

## Remaining risks

- The narrowed attribution assumes a rejected STOR leaves the destination control channel in
  sync. That is the same assumption the dupe-skip path has relied on in production for months;
  the spread pools' validate-on-borrow remains the safety net for any mis-narrowed session.
- The 425 rejections themselves are remote and continue at about 1%; this release reduces their
  cost (one login and one reconnect per event), not their count.
- SYN's FTP endpoint remains unreachable; the 5-minute remount warnings continue until the
  remote service or its configuration is corrected.
- The v3.10.108 relocation recovery branch still awaits its first natural production trigger.
- Working set on v3.10.108 was 308 MB after 9 h (private bytes 186 MB), consistent with the
  post-v3.10.106 baseline; a 24–48 h sample on v3.10.109 is still needed.
